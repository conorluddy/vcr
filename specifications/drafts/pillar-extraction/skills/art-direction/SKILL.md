# Art direction

Draft, advisory and uncalibrated. Links are deployment-relative: they target the final `skills/art-direction/SKILL.md` location.

Look up applicable rule definitions in the referenced section or canonical rules data before applying a limit; never use thresholds from memory. Read one section at a time and preserve provisional status.

Start from the selected workspace brief, delivery aspects, focal intent and any existing series invariants. Scale the work to the request: reuse settled direction for a local revision, explore only unresolved choices, and make style frames where they help settle or communicate those choices. Consult research only where it informs an open decision; distinguish audience-scoped interpretations from assumptions. Keep choices and any declared breaks in the workspace.

Identify the decision and its constraints first. If an essential constraint is missing, state a working assumption and its consequence, or ask when the choice cannot responsibly be made. For a revision, inspect the supplied frame and identify the competing visual weights before changing it; preserve unrelated settled elements. For new direction, compare a small number of materially different visual approaches when the choice is open, then make a concrete recommendation tied to the brief. Use actual requested aspects and crops, not an assumed portrait format. If a scratch specimen needs dimensions that were not supplied, label them as specimen-only and never as a delivery requirement; the actual aspect and crop remain open.

State the organising visual principle, how it serves the brief, and which visible choices express it. Translate it into palette roles, shape language, grid and alignment principles, and focal hierarchy. Sketch value and focal hierarchy before refining colour and texture. Explore representative moments at their delivery aspects; identify what stays consistent and where variation is intentional. Record the rationale and exclusions behind any reference transfer. Justify visual choices against the brief; familiarity alone is neither a defect nor an identity.

Across beats, specify where attention should land and whether the focal point should persist or move. Check that adjacent compositions support that progression; hand its timing and movement to pacing and motion rather than prescribing their execution.

Read only the needed route in [art direction](../../references/art-director/README.md): §1–2 for palette roles and colour tolerances, §3 for design choices that survive delivery, §4–5 for value and composition, §7–8 for visual language and series, §9 for covers and crops, §11 for distinctiveness, §15 for creation. `ART-COL-*`, `ART-CMP-*`, `ART-GEN-*` and `ART-COV-*` are provisional reference IDs. Safe placement belongs to `TYPO-SAFE`; text contrast to `TYPO-CONTRAST`; graphic contrast and colour-independent meaning to `A11Y-*`. Delivery owns encoded colour checks.

Self-check hierarchy in greyscale and at intended viewing size, compare representative frames and crops with the brief, and inspect the delivered image where available. No automated art-direction check exists; distinguish observation from measurement and flag missing evidence or unverified heuristics. Make the applicable decisions identifiable in existing workspace notes: palette roles, shape language, grid and alignment, focal progression, invariants and permitted variation. Hand these decisions, any style frames and open risks to typography, motion, pacing, image direction and delivery as relevant; no separate document format is required. A reviewer remains independent and advisory.

Report what was changed or proposed, why it serves the focal intent, what was actually inspected and what remains unverified. A source frame or static sketch cannot establish temporal continuity, encoded colour survival or performance at an unspecified viewing size. Do not call a visual judgement a measured pass or treat a reference heuristic as a gate.
