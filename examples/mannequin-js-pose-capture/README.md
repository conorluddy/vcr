# mannequin-js pose capture

Status: partial, on 2026-11-24, macOS arm64. Nine of ten predeclared checks pass; the wrist-displacement check (check 5) fails: the right wrist moved 0.404 library units against a predeclared 1.0. The sections below are the plan written before the first run, followed by what was observed. `expected/` is empty because `run.sh --record` stops at a failing check, so the evidence scope is "Fixture-reported".

## Intent and scope

- Tool: `mannequin-js` (npm `mannequin-js` 5.2.3, GPL-3.0), a procedural articulated human figure built on Three.js. Used with `three` 0.186.1 (exact pin, no peer dependency declared by the package; its own examples use three 0.169 from a CDN) and `playwright` through `@vcr/capture`.
- VCR job and claim: `mannequin-js` creates a figure whose joint angles (torso bend, right arm raise, left knee bend) are set as pure functions of seek time t. Seeked frames at predeclared times match between shuffled and sequential capture; the poses at predeclared times differ; and the right wrist's world position, read through the library's joints and Three.js `getWorldPosition`, moves by a predeclared amount between two poses.
- Explicit exclusions:
  - No IK, no balance or ground contact physics beyond the library's one-shot `stepOnGround()` at setup, no collisions.
  - Not exercised: the library's posture editor, `posture` strings and `blend()`, labels (`TextGeometry`), female or child bodies (`feminine = false`, height 1 only), finger and head joints, custom body parts, `OrbitControls`, the library's ground and shadow stage (`createStage` is not called).
  - Not a claim about the figure's anatomy, skin, or realism; not a claim about GPUs or browsers other than the capture route's Chromium.
  - Licence GPL-3.0 applies to the library. This example reads its source from `node_modules` and copies none of it into the repository.
- Pose is rebuilt from t on every call. The three angles are absolute values computed from t and written to the joints; no state is stepped, so a single jump to any time gives the same pose as a sequential walk.
  - `torso.bend` = 30 × smoothstep(t / 1.0) degrees.
  - `r_arm.raise` = -5 + 90 × smoothstep(t / 1.5) degrees.
  - `l_knee.bend` = 50 × smoothstep((t - 1.5) / 1.0) degrees.
  - smoothstep(x) = x²(3 − 2x) on x clamped to [0, 1].
- Library clock and loop: `mannequin-js/scene.js` starts its own `setAnimationLoop` with a `THREE.Clock`. The scene stops that loop (`setAnimationLoop(null)`) and never calls `getElapsedTime`. Frames come only from `seek(t)`.
- Renderer: the library's renderer is created without `alpha`, so it would paint an opaque background. The scene draws the library's scene graph (`scene`, `camera`) with its own alpha renderer and clears the scene background to transparent. The library's renderer and canvas are disposed and removed.
- Sources and terms:
  - Library: https://boytchev.github.io/mannequin.js/ and https://github.com/boytchev/mannequin.js. Licence GPL-3.0, read from the npm tarball `package.json` and `LICENSE` on 2026-11-24. npm `latest` is 5.2.3. The tool card dates that release 2026-10-06; the npm registry's package-level `time.modified` reads 2024-10-06. The release date was not re-verified here.
  - three: MIT.
  - No external assets: the figure is generated by the library, and the font is embedded in `src/font.js`. The scene uses no network.
- Unseeded features: a grep of `node_modules/mannequin-js/src` for `Math.random`, `performance.now` and `Date.now` returns no matches. In `three` 0.186.1 the code is split: `build/three.module.js` has none of the three, while `build/three.core.js` has 6 `performance.now` (inside `THREE.Clock`, which only the stopped library loop calls) and 21 `Math.random` (UUID, random vector and math helpers, none called by this scene or by mannequin-js). `Date.now` has no matches. Nothing unseeded reaches the frames.
- Environment: macOS (Darwin 25.6.0), arm64, Chromium from the capture route (recorded in the observation).
- Input: none. The figure is generated in the page.
- Dependencies and installation: `mannequin-js` 5.2.3 and `three` 0.186.1 pinned exactly in `examples/mannequin-js-pose-capture/package.json`, installed with `pnpm install` at the repository root. The browser loads the library from `./node_modules/mannequin-js/src/` through an import map; no bundler. The installed package has no browser build of its own, and its `package.json` `main` points at a file that is absent, so the import map targets `src/` directly.
- Workspace and output: `examples/mannequin-js-pose-capture/output/` (git-ignored). Expected results: `expected/summary.json` and `expected/probe.json` only, no media.

## Plan, before running

Commands:

```text
pnpm install                                                  # repo root, once
examples/mannequin-js-pose-capture/run.sh --record            # captures, probes, records expected/
examples/mannequin-js-pose-capture/run.sh                     # must print mannequin-js-pose-capture: OK
```

Versions: mannequin-js 5.2.3, three 0.186.1, @vcr/capture (Playwright, version recorded at run time), Node via tsx 4.23.15.

Capture: 1280x720, 30 fps, duration 3 s, 90 frames, `verifyDeterminism: true` (sequential capture, then shuffled capture, compared frame by frame).

Probe (`probe.ts`): reads `window.__scene.meta` and `window.__scene.probe(t)` at t = 0, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.5 s. The probe reads back `torso.bend`, `r_arm.raise` and `l_knee.bend` through the library's joint getters, and `r_wrist.getWorldPosition` through Three.js. Values are rounded to 6 decimal places. The figure height in library units is recorded in the probe as a measurement, not predeclared.

Predeclared checks (any failure makes `run.sh` exit non-zero):

1. Figure built: `figureHeightUnits` > 0.
2. Torso bend: 0 at t = 0; 30 degrees at t = 1.0, 1.5 and 2.5 (tolerance 1e-6).
3. Right arm raise: -5 degrees at t = 0; 61.6667 degrees at t = 1.0 (tolerance 0.001); 85 degrees at t = 1.5 and 2.5.
4. Left knee bend: 0 degrees at t = 0, 1.0 and 1.5; 50 degrees at t = 2.5.
5. Wrist: the right wrist's world position moves at least 1.0 library unit between t = 0 and t = 1.5 (Euclidean distance, `getWorldPosition`).
6. Frames: 90 frames at 1280x720; shuffled order matches sequential.
7. Hashes: frames 0000 (t = 0), 0030 (t = 1.0), 0045 (t = 1.5) and 0075 (t = 2.5) are all distinct.
8. Knee-only change: frame 0045 vs 0075 differs in at least one pixel (`magick compare -metric AE`).
9. Knee-only change is smaller than the torso-and-arm change: AE(0045, 0075) < AE(0000, 0030).
10. Alpha: frame 0030 corner pixel (0,0) has alpha 0, and its alpha maximum is 1 (some opaque pixels).

Contact sheet: frames 0000, 0015, 0030, 0045, 0060, 0075 (90-frame layout). Built by hand after the run, not by `run.sh`:

```text
cd examples/mannequin-js-pose-capture/output/frames && magick \( frame-0000.png frame-0015.png frame-0030.png +append +repage \) \( frame-0045.png frame-0060.png frame-0075.png +append +repage \) -append +repage -background '#808080' -flatten -resize 50% ../contact.png
```

Inspection: the figure must be complete in frame, unclipped, lit, and visibly posed (torso leaning, right arm raised, left knee bent by t = 2.5 s). A blank, clipped or unposed figure is `fail` whatever the hashes say.

Stop conditions and result mapping:
- Capture or probe throws (for example, a module fails to load in the page): `fail`, with the error.
- Checks 1 to 7 and 10 pass, but check 9 fails: `partial`, since the claim's pose-differs part holds and only the relative magnitude does not.
- Any other check fails, or the contact sheet shows a blank, clipped or unposed figure: `fail`.
- `blocked` only if the pinned packages cannot be installed or the page cannot be served.

Reproduction: `run.sh`, then inspect `output/probe.json`, `output/summary.json`, `output/report.json` and the contact sheet.

Result: partial. Three of the four claim parts hold: the joints are set as pure functions of t (readbacks match), seeked frames match between shuffled and sequential capture, and the poses differ (four distinct frames, the knee change is measurable). The fourth part, that a joint's world position moves by a predeclared amount, holds only in direction: the wrist moved 0.40 units, not the predeclared 1.0.

Deviations and limitations:
- Mannequin's library renderer is opaque, so frames come from a separate alpha renderer that draws the library's scene. The library's loop and clock are stopped, and its ground and shadow stage (`createStage`) is not used.
- Torso bend and arm raise are set from absolute values each call; the library's default posture (for example torso bend 2) is overwritten at t = 0 by 0.
- Only one figure (male, height 1) and three joints are exercised; the claim is about these joints.
- Nothing is reported about other browsers, GPUs or hosts. Hashes are from this host.

What this result establishes and does not establish:
- Establishes: mannequin-js 5.2.3 builds a figure in three 0.186.1 that loads in Chromium 156 without a bundler, takes absolute joint angles from t, and renders deterministic, distinct, alpha-transparent frames on this host.
- Does not establish: the magnitude of the wrist motion against the predeclared threshold (failed), realistic motion, IK, other joints, other figure types, other hosts, or the GPL-3.0 implications of using the library.
