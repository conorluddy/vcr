# Rubber Band time-stretch and pitch-shift of generated tones and clicks

Status: fail for the claim as worded, 2026-11-24 (see "Observation, after running"). Duration checks pass; the pitch-keeping and octave checks fail on FFT peak, and reruns are not byte-identical across invocations. Not validated; no `expected/` recorded. Checked 2026-11-24.

## Intent and scope

- Tool: `rubberband` 4.0.0 (Homebrew bottle, `/opt/homebrew/bin/rubberband`, already installed on this machine when checked; `rubberband-r3` is also present and is not tested here). Engine R2, selected explicitly with `-2`.
- Licence: GPL-2.0-or-later (`brew info --json=v2 rubberband`, checked 2026-11-24). The project page (https://breakfastquay.com/rubberband/) says the library is GPL and that proprietary distribution needs a commercial licence. VCR runs the CLI locally and ships nothing.
- Documentation: https://breakfastquay.com/rubberband/ (v4.0.0 released 2024-10-25, per the page; `rubberband --help` for the options used here).
- VCR job and claim: `rubberband` time-stretches a generated 2.000 s signal by a predeclared ratio of 1.5 to the predeclared duration of 3.000 s, within a predeclared tolerance, and keeps the tone's pitch within a predeclared cents tolerance, measured by FFT peak. It also pitch-shifts a generated 440 Hz tone by +12 semitones, so its FFT peak moves to 880 Hz within the cents tolerance, and keeps its duration. Reruns of each command are recorded as byte-identical or not; that is recorded either way and is not a gate.
- Two inputs, both generated by `ffmpeg` `aevalsrc` (no third-party media): a 440 Hz sine (`tone-440.wav`) and a click track (`click-track.wav`, 4 ms 1 kHz bursts every 0.25 s, first at 0.20 s, eight bursts). The click track checks that transients stay at the predicted times after stretching.
- Pillars (cited for context only, not validated here): `SOUND-SFX-REPEAT` in `references/sound-design/` (repeated samples vary in pitch and level, which a pitch shift can supply). Nothing here tests musical quality, pacing or mix.
- Exclusions: no listening or perceptual quality claim; no claim about formant preservation (`-F` is not used); no claim about the R3 engine (`rubberband-r3`) or the realtime/timemap/pitchmap options; no other sample rates, channel counts or durations; no claim about other platforms or Rubber Band versions; no claim about the FFmpeg `rubberband` filter (it is not in FFmpeg 9.0.2 on this machine, checked with `ffmpeg -filters`, so no second route is recorded); no tool-to-tool edge.
- Mapping to the template's checks: "frame count" is the output sample count; "dimensions" is sample rate, channel count and bit depth; "at least two distinct hashes" is applied as three distinct output hashes for the three routes; "shuffled order equals sequential" does not apply because there is no seeking, so the rerun byte check stands in for determinism; "alpha range" does not apply (audio). A waveform or spectrogram image is inspected by eye for shape only.
- Environment: macOS 26.6 arm64 (Darwin 25.6.0), Node v22.19.0, Homebrew rubberband 4.0.0, ffmpeg and ffprobe 9.0.2. CPU only. No network use by `run.sh`.
- Input: none committed. `run.sh` generates both inputs deterministically with `aevalsrc` (pure sine and gated sine expressions, no random source).
- Dependencies and installation: `rubberband` is the only new catalogue dependency and is already installed. The install recipe on the record is `brew install rubberband`. `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` (catalogue tool `ffmpeg-ffprobe`) generate the inputs and cross-check sample counts. `jq`, `shasum` and `cmp` are system or Homebrew tools. `analyse.ts` is run with the repository's existing `tsx` and uses only Node built-ins. No npm package is added to this example.
- Workspace and cleanup: writes only `output/` (git-ignored). Remove with `rm -rf examples/rubber-band-stretch/output`.
- Catalogue tools used by the fixture: `rubber-band` (the claim) and `ffmpeg-ffprobe` (input generation and the sample-count cross-check).

## Plan, before running

- Versions to record: `rubberband --version` (4.0.0), `ffmpeg -version` and `ffprobe -version` (9.0.2), `node -v`, `jq --version`, and the Homebrew formula version.
- Inputs (`run.sh`), 48 kHz mono, 2.000 s, written as 32-bit float WAV:
  - `output/tone-440.wav`: `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc=0.5*sin(2*PI*440*t):s=48000:d=2" -ac 1 -c:a pcm_f32le`.
  - `output/click-track.wav`: `aevalsrc=0.8*sin(2*PI*1000*t)*lt(mod(t+0.05\,0.25)\,0.004):s=48000:d=2`. Bursts start at 0.20 + 0.25k s for k = 0..7.
- Routes (`run.sh`), each run twice (`-a` and `-b`) into `output/`:
  - Stretch: `rubberband -2 -q -t 1.5 <in> <out>` on `tone-440.wav` and on `click-track.wav`. Expected output 3.000 s (144000 samples); expected click onsets at 0.30 + 0.375k s.
  - Pitch shift: `rubberband -2 -q -p 12 tone-440.wav <out>`. Expected output 2.000 s (96000 samples) at 880 Hz.
- Analysis (`analyse.ts`, Node built-ins, strict TypeScript): reads 16-bit PCM or 32-bit float WAV, takes channel 0, computes an FFT peak (Hann window, zero-padded to a power of two, parabolic interpolation on log magnitude) over 20 Hz to 20 kHz, and detects click onsets from 1 ms RMS windows crossing 0.1 with a 0.1 s refractory gap. `ffprobe` gives `duration_ts` as a cross-check on sample count.
- Predeclared gating checks (any failure exits 1 and the summary is not diffed):
  1. Input tone: 48000 Hz, 1 channel, 96000 samples, FFT peak within 1 cent of 440 Hz (analyser sanity).
  2. Input clicks: exactly 8 onsets, each within 2 ms of 0.20 + 0.25k s.
  3. Stretch tone: 48000 Hz, 1 channel, 144000 samples within 10 ms (480 samples) of 3.000 s; FFT peak within 5 cents of 440 Hz.
  4. Stretch clicks: 144000 samples within 10 ms; exactly 8 onsets; each onset within 10 ms of 0.30 + 0.375k s.
  5. Pitch tone: 48000 Hz, 1 channel, 96000 samples within 10 ms of 2.000 s (duration kept); FFT peak within 5 cents of 880 Hz (exactly one octave up).
  6. `ffprobe` `duration_ts` equals the analyser's sample count for every output.
  7. Distinct hashes: the three `-a` outputs have three distinct SHA-256 values, none equal to an input hash.
  8. `expected/summary.json` matches `output/summary.json` exactly (`diff`).
- Recorded, not gating (the claim says "record either way"): for each route, whether the `-a` and `-b` outputs are byte-identical (`cmp` and SHA-256). A false value is reported as nondeterminism in the observation, and the summary still records it.
- Stop conditions: `rubberband`, `ffmpeg`, `ffprobe` or `jq` missing: report blocked. Any gating check failing: report fail (or partial if only the rerun or a secondary check is off), with the failing check named. Do not widen a tolerance after seeing a result.
- Reproduce: `examples/rubber-band-stretch/run.sh --record`, then `examples/rubber-band-stretch/run.sh` (expect `rubber-band-stretch: OK`). Inspect `output/contact.png` (ffmpeg `showwavespic` waveforms of the inputs and outputs, stacked with ImageMagick `magick -append`) and `output/summary.json`.

## Observation, after running

- Executor and date: Conor's MacBook Pro (`Conors-MacBook-Pro-2.local`), macOS 26.6 arm64 (Darwin 25.6.0), 2026-11-24, from the `rubber-band-stretch` worktree.
- Versions: rubberband 4.0.0 (`rubberband --version`; Homebrew formula 4.0.0 was already installed, so no install was run; engine R2 via `-2`). ffmpeg and ffprobe 9.0.2. FFmpeg has no `rubberband` filter (`ffmpeg -filters` lists none), so only the CLI route ran. Node v22.19.0 with the repository's `tsx`. jq 1.8.1. ImageMagick 7.1.2-35 for the stacked waveform only. No Playwright or Chromium involved.
- Commands: `run.sh --record` exited 1 at the gate (predeclared checks failed, so nothing was recorded to `expected/`). A plain `run.sh` run also exited 1 with the same three checks failing (stretch tone, stretch clicks, pitch tone), so the failure reproduces. No `OK` was printed.
- Checks (run 2 values; run 1 matched on every gating field at 6 dp):
  1. Input tone, 48000 Hz mono, 96000 samples, FFT peak 439.999978 Hz: pass.
  2. Input clicks: exactly 8 onsets at 0.200 + 0.250k s, each within 2 ms: pass.
  3. Stretch tone: 144000 samples (3.000 s): pass. FFT peak 442.729842 Hz, +10.71 cents against 440 Hz; limit 5 cents: **fail** (both routes, a and b).
  4. Stretch clicks: 144000 samples: pass. Eight onsets, spacing 0.375 s, but each sits 12 to 14 ms early against 1.5 × input onsets (0.286, 0.661, 1.037, 1.412, 1.787, 2.163, 2.536, 2.911 s); limit 10 ms: **fail**.
  5. Pitch-shifted tone (+12 semitones): 96000 samples (2.000 s): pass. FFT peak 872.941496 Hz, −13.94 cents against 880 Hz; limit 5 cents: **fail**.
  6. ffprobe `duration_ts` and sample rate agree with analyser for the five a-run files: pass.
  7. Distinct hashes: three a-run outputs distinct and none equal to an input: pass.
  8. Reruns (recorded, not gating): within each `run.sh` invocation, the a and b outputs were byte-identical for all three routes (both invocations). Across invocations the outputs were not: the stretched-tone SHA-256 was `75a668da…` in the record run and `1c6f1769…` in the plain run, and inputs were identical. Standalone, three direct `rubberband -2 -q -t 1.5` runs gave two identical files and a third that differed from the first in 56599 of 144000 samples (maximum difference 0.77, sample range ±0.79). Byte identity is therefore not reliable on this host.
- Why the frequency checks fail (verified outside `run.sh` with numpy, scratch only): the stretched tone's 1 s Hann spectrum has its strongest lines at 442.75 Hz (3361) and 438.40 Hz (2837), with 440 Hz weak (531 against 3510 at 442.6 Hz). Further lines at 434.4 and 444.6 Hz. The pitch-shifted tone has its strongest lines at 872.96 Hz and 887.05 Hz, with the 880 Hz bin at 52 against 3508. Zero-crossing estimates of the stretched tone land near 440 Hz, which is why an averaged estimate disagrees with the FFT peak. The FFT method is the predeclared one and was not changed.
- Inspection: `output/contact.png`, five rows of waveforms at 1200 px wide. Row 1 (input tone) is a steady band. Rows 2 and 3 (stretched and pitch-shifted tones) show regular dips and gating across the whole length, so the sine is amplitude-modulated, not clean. Row 4 (input clicks) shows eight sharp bursts. Row 5 (stretched clicks) shows eight bursts at about the predicted spacing, each with pre-echo smear before the peak. The output is not blank and not clipped, and it shows the library's work, but it does not show a clean pitch-preserved tone.
- Result: **fail** for the claim as worded. Duration is exact for both routes (144000 and 96000 samples, confirmed by ffprobe). Pitch is not kept within 5 cents on the stretch (+10.7 cents) and the octave shift is off by −13.9 cents, with the carrier largely replaced by sidebands. Click spacing holds, but the absolute onset offset exceeds 10 ms. Reruns are not byte-identical across invocations.
- Deviations and limitations: only the R2 engine (`-2`) was run; `rubberband-r3` and the `-F` formant option were not tested, and a different engine could behave differently. The tolerances were not widened after seeing the result. No listening test was done. The result holds for these inputs (generated steady sine at 440 Hz and a 1 kHz gated click train), at 48 kHz mono float32, ratio 1.5, +12 semitones.
- What this establishes: rubberband 4.0.0 R2, invoked as in `run.sh`, returns the predicted sample counts for a 1.5× stretch and a +12-semitone shift of a 2 s generated signal, and its clicks keep their spacing. It does not establish that the output keeps tone pitch within 5 cents on steady sines, that the outputs are byte-identical across invocations, or that the onset timing is within 10 ms. It is not evidence about FFmpeg's (absent) rubberband filter, the R3 engine, quality or musical use.
- Reviewer, follow-up and revisit triggers: a follow-up could repeat with `rubberband-r3`, with the R2 `--crisp` or formant options, or with a non-steady tone; any such run needs its own predeclared plan. Revisit if the Homebrew build changes, or if a rerun shows the same outputs twice in a row across separate invocations. The pillar `SOUND-SFX-REPEAT` treats pitch variation as useful, so the sideband artefact matters for that use; no pillar threshold was tested here.
