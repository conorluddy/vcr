# Camera direction within image direction

Reference-only craft guidance, reviewed 2026-11-07. Image Direction owns the image requirement and viewpoint; Story owns coverage and sequence; Motion owns camera movement; Lighting and Materials own appearance. This extends the existing pillar rather than creating a twentieth review stage. Recommendations below are heuristics, not automatic gates.

## Choose what the viewer must see

State the relation that must remain visible, then choose shot scale, camera position, height and viewing angle. Compare an establishing view, a relation-revealing view and a detail view at final placement. Select only the coverage the concept needs. High or low viewpoints do not have universal emotional meanings; route consequential associations to Cultural Context.

## Viewpoint and lens

Record camera position separately from focal length and sensor/filmback or field of view. To compare lenses, state whether position or subject framing was held fixed. Changing lens while also moving the camera changes two variables; do not attribute the result to lens choice alone. For constructed imagery, specify the visible perspective and compare test views rather than relying on a focal-length adjective.

Nikon's [focal-length guide](https://www.nikon.co.uk/en_GB/learn-and-explore/magazine/tips-and-tricks/what-is-focal-length-in-photography-a-guide-for-beginners) explains angle of view and subject magnification. Read 2026-11-07. Its “compression” summaries are practical descriptions, not a controlled demonstration separating viewpoint from focal length. No universal lens choice is prescribed here.

## Focus and depth of field

State which planes must remain understandable. Shallow focus may separate a subject but hide a relationship; deep focus may preserve relationships but increase clutter. Review the important details at delivery size and throughout motion, including focus transitions. Columbia's [depth-of-field glossary](https://filmglossary.ccnmtl.columbia.edu/term/depth-of-field/) defines the region of acceptable focus; it does not establish a creative optimum. Page read 2026-11-07.

## Continuity and coverage

For a connected sequence, record screen positions, travel direction, gaze target and camera-side relationship. Review adjacent shots in order; an isolated attractive frame cannot establish continuity. The [180-degree convention](https://filmglossary.ccnmtl.columbia.edu/term/180-degree-rule/) helps preserve screen geography, while an [eyeline match](https://filmglossary.ccnmtl.columbia.edu/term/eye-line-match/) relates a look to its target. Both pages read 2026-11-07. These are conventions that can be intentionally broken; the first source explicitly discusses expressive departures. Record the intended effect and inspect whether geography remains legible.

## Handoff

Workspace shot notes retain: purpose; shot scale; position/height/angle; field of view or lens plus filmback when known; focus target and required planes; screen direction and gaze; crop variants; camera/focus motion; observed reference locator; inferred settings labelled unknown; selected test view and rejected alternative. Do not infer unseen settings from a finished image.

Art Direction judges composition. Image Direction tests candidate fit and crop survival. Story tests coverage and visual logic. Motion tests movement and temporal continuity. Use existing owning rules for safe zones, reading holds and accessibility; this document introduces no numeric thresholds or camera simulator.
